[Peter Lombard](/glossary/names#peter) was born around 1096 in the Lombardy region of northern Italy to a poor family. Through the patronage of Odo, Bishop of Lucca, and the encouragement of [St. Bernard of Clairvaux](/people/saints/medieval/bernard-of-clairvaux), he continued his studies in France, eventually settling in Paris. There he taught at the cathedral school of Notre-Dame, earning recognition as one of the foremost theologians of his generation through his learning and academic ability.

Ordained successively as a subdeacon, deacon, archdeacon, and priest, Lombard devoted himself to teaching theology. Following the example of earlier scholars such as [St. Isidore of Seville](/people/saints/latin-fathers/isidore-of-seville), he compiled the *Four Books of <Definition lang="latin" anchor="sententia" text="Sentences" />*, a systematic collection of biblical texts and the writings of the Church Fathers, arranged to present the whole of Christian doctrine.

Completed in the 1150s, the *Sentences* became the standard textbook of theology in the medieval universities and the foundation of scholastic theology for the next four centuries. From the 13th to the 16th century, no work of Christian literature apart from the Bible was commented upon more frequently. It shaped theological education throughout the Middle Ages and was studied by figures such as [St. Thomas Aquinas](/people/saints/medieval/thomas-aquinas), [William of Ockham](/people/medieval-theologians/william-of-ockham), [Martin Luther](/meditations/a-brief-history-of-the-reformation-part-3#martin-luther), and [John Calvin](/meditations/a-brief-history-of-the-reformation-part-3#john-calvin).

On July 28, 1159, the feast of Saints [Peter](/people/saints/apostles/peter) and [Paul](/people/saints/apostles/paul), Peter Lombard was consecrated Bishop of Paris. His episcopate was brief, as he died the following year, but his *Sentences* secured his enduring reputation as the "Master of the Sentences" and one of the most influential theologians of the Middle Ages.
