<Initial text="Lanfranc of Pavia" />

Lanfranc was born about 1005 at Pavia in northern Italy. After receiving a distinguished education in the liberal arts, he crossed the Alps and established himself as a teacher at Avranches in Normandy. About 1042 he abandoned his promising secular career and entered the newly founded Benedictine Abbey of Bec. He was appointed prior a few years later and established a school whose fame attracted scholars from throughout Europe.

As prior, Lanfranc became one of the foremost teachers and theologians of his age. Among his pupils was [St. Anselm](/people/saints/medieval/anselm-of-canterbury), who later succeeded him both at Bec and Canterbury. Lanfranc employed his mastery of logic in the exposition of Holy Scripture and in defense of the Church's doctrine of the Eucharist against Berengar of Tours. His principal theological work, *On the Body and Blood of the Lord*, affirmed the real presence of Christ in the consecrated bread and wine.

Lanfranc also became a trusted adviser to William the Conqueror, who appointed him the first abbot of the newly founded Abbey of Saint Stephen at Caen in 1066. Following the Norman Conquest, he reluctantly accepted appointment as Archbishop of Canterbury and was consecrated in 1070. As archbishop, he reorganized the English Church, held councils for the reform of clerical and monastic discipline, and strengthened the authority of Canterbury. He rebuilt Canterbury Cathedral after its destruction by fire in 1067, restored and carefully administered the lands belonging to the archbishopric, founded hospitals for the sick and poor, and gave generously to widows and others in need.

As William the Conqueror's representative in England, Lanfranc helped suppress the Revolt of the Earls in 1075. After William's death, he supported the succession of William Rufus but admonished the new king to govern justly. In May 1089, Lanfranc was stricken with fever. On May 24, before taking a prescribed medicine, he asked first to make his confession and receive the holy [Viaticum](/glossary/liturgical-terms#viaticum); afterward he took the cup in his hand but, before drinking, calmly breathed his last. He was buried in Canterbury Cathedral, having laid the foundations upon which St. Anselm would further strengthen the English Church.
