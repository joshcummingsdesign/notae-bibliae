[Boethius](/glossary/names#boethius) was born around 480 AD in Rome to a wealthy Christian family during the collapse of the Western Roman Empire. Orphaned at a young age, he was adopted by the patrician Quintus Aurelius Memmius Symmachus, who provided him with an excellent education in philosophy and literature. Boethius mastered Greek, a rare accomplishment in the West, and devoted himself to preserving the intellectual heritage of classical antiquity.

One of the leading Christian scholars of the 6th century, Boethius sought to reconcile the philosophy of Plato and Aristotle with Christian theology. He translated and commented on numerous Greek and Latin works, wrote on music, mathematics, and theology, and envisioned making the whole of Greek learning available to the Latin West. Through his translations and commentaries, he helped preserve the works of Aristotle for later generations.

Inspired by Plato's *Republic*, Boethius entered public service under Theodoric the Great, rising rapidly to become senator, consul, and eventually *magister officiorum*, the highest administrative office in the Ostrogothic kingdom. His efforts to oppose corruption at court, however, led to his imprisonment on charges of conspiracy around 523 AD.

While awaiting execution, Boethius composed his masterpiece, *On the Consolation of Philosophy*, a profound reflection on providence, suffering, and the nature of true happiness that became one of the most influential books of the early Middle Ages. He was executed in 524 AD and is remembered as a Christian martyr.
