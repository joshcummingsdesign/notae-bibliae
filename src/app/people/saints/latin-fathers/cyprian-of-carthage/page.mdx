**Bishop and Martyr** (258 AD)

[St. Cyprian](/glossary/names#cyprian) was born at Carthage and spent much of his life as a pagan teacher of rhetoric before being converted to Christianity through the influence of the priest Caecilian. After his baptism, he gave his possessions to the poor, was ordained priest, and soon afterward became Bishop of Carthage. During the Decian persecution, he temporarily withdrew from the city for the sake of his flock, and afterward worked to restore those who had denied the faith but sincerely repented, while maintaining the discipline of the Church. When persecution resumed under Emperor Valerian, Cyprian was banished to Curubis, where he continued to preach, and was later brought back to Carthage. Refusing the proconsul’s command to sacrifice to the Roman gods, he calmly accepted his sentence with the words, “God be thanked,” and suffered martyrdom in 258 AD, bearing his final witness to Christ in the presence of his flock.
