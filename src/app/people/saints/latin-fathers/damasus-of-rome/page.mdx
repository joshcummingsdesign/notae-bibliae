**Bishop** (384 AD)

[St. Damasus](/glossary/names#damasus) was born around 305 AD to Christian parents from Lusitania (modern Portugal) and was raised in Rome, where his father served as a priest at the Church of [St. Lawrence](/people/saints/early/lawrence-of-rome) (San Lorenzo). Beginning his own ministry as a deacon and later a priest, Damasus rose to prominence in the Roman Church and helped oversee its affairs during the exile of Pope Liberius.

Following the death of Liberius in 366 AD, Damasus was elected Bishop of Rome amid a bitter disputed election with Ursinus that led to violence and civil unrest. Despite this troubled beginning, he became a steadfast defender of Nicene orthodoxy, opposing Arianism and other contemporary heresies, including Apollinarianism and Macedonianism. He also supported the First Council of Constantinople in 381 AD through his representatives.

Damasus strengthened the position of the Roman See and presided over the Council of Rome in 382 AD, which affirmed the canon of Sacred Scripture recognized in the Western Church. He also appointed [St. Jerome](/people/saints/latin-fathers/jerome-of-stridon) as his secretary and commissioned him to revise the [Vetus Latina](/bible/translations/vetus-latina), a work that would culminate in the Latin [Vulgate](/bible/translations/vulgate) and profoundly shape Western Christianity.

Throughout his pontificate, Damasus promoted the veneration of the martyrs by restoring and embellishing their tombs in the Roman catacombs. He also restored his father's church, the Church of St. Lawrence, which by the seventh century had become an important stop on pilgrimages to the graves of the Roman martyrs. He died in 384 AD, leaving a lasting legacy in the worship, biblical scholarship, and memory of the Latin Church.
