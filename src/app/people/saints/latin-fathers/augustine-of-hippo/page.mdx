**Bishop** (430 AD)

[St. Augustine](/glossary/names#augustine) was born in 354 AD at Thagaste in North Africa to Patricius, a pagan father, and St. Monica, whose patient prayers and example profoundly shaped his life. After studying rhetoric at Carthage and living for many years under the influence of Manichaeism and other philosophies, he came under the preaching of [St. Ambrose](/people/saints/latin-fathers/ambrose-of-milan) at Milan. Convicted by the reading of Scripture after hearing the words, "Take up and read," Augustine was converted and baptized by Ambrose at Easter in 387 AD. Returning to North Africa, he was ordained priest and later became Bishop of Hippo, where he devoted himself to preaching, pastoral ministry, and writing. In around 400 AD, he composed the *Rule of Saint Augustine* for communities living a common life of prayer, charity, and service. He defended the Church against the [Donatists](/glossary/historical-terms#donatism) and [Pelagians](/glossary/historical-terms#pelagianism), and composed such enduring works as his *Confessions*, *On the Trinity*, and *The City of God*. Augustine grew fatally ill during the Vandal siege of Hippo, and died in 430 AD. He is remembered as one of the great theologians and Fathers of the Latin Church.
