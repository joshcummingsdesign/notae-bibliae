**Priest** (420 AD)

[St. Jerome](/glossary/names#jerome) was born around 331 AD at Stridon, on the borders of Dalmatia and Pannonia, and was educated in rhetoric and law at Rome. After turning increasingly to sacred studies, he withdrew into the Syrian desert, where he embraced the monastic life and undertook the difficult study of Hebrew. He was later ordained priest at Antioch and spent several years at Constantinople under the influence of [St. Gregory of Nazianzus](/people/saints/cappadocian-fathers/gregory-of-nazianzus), translating the writings of [Eusebius](/people/early-christian-writers/eusebius) and [Origen](/people/early-christian-writers/origen-of-alexandria) into Latin. Returning to Rome in 382 AD, Jerome became secretary to [Pope St. Damasus](/people/saints/latin-fathers/damasus-of-rome), who employed his considerable learning and knowledge of Hebrew in revising the existing Latin translations of Scripture. After leaving Rome, he eventually settled at Bethlehem, where he established a monastery and devoted the remainder of his life to the translation and commentary of Scripture. There he produced the Latin translation of the Bible known as the [Vulgate](/bible/translations/vulgate), the work for which he is chiefly remembered. St. Jerome died at Bethlehem in 419 AD, and was buried there in the cave adjoining his monastery.
