**Bishop** (521 AD)

[St. Jacob of Serugh](/glossary/names#jacob) was born in 451 AD in the region of Serugh on the Euphrates and was educated at the renowned School of Edessa. He served as <Definition lang="greek" anchor="khorepiskopos" text="chorepiscopus" />, overseeing the rural churches of the Serugh district during a period of war and hardship caused by the invasions of the Sasanian Empire.

Jacob lived through the Christological controversies that followed the Council of Chalcedon. While the council affirmed that Christ is one Person in two natures, Jacob held to a moderate form of [Miaphysitism](/glossary/historical-terms#miaphysitism) common in the Syriac tradition of his time. Although he held this position, he took practically no part in the Christological controversies of his day, devoting himself instead to pastoral ministry, study, and literature.

In 519 AD, at the age of sixty-seven, Jacob was elected Bishop of Batnan. He became one of the foremost poets and theologians of the Syriac Christian tradition, second only to [St. Ephrem the Syrian](/people/saints/syriac-fathers/ephrem-the-syrian). During his lifetime he composed 763 works, around 400 of which survive, earning him enduring titles such as the "Flute of the Holy Spirit" and the "Harp of the Church."

Jacob's writings, especially his metrical homilies, were among the most influential in Syriac Christianity and survive in more manuscripts than those of nearly any other writer of late antiquity, surpassed only by [St. Augustine](/people/saints/latin-fathers/augustine-of-hippo) and [St. John Chrysostom](/people/saints/greek-fathers/john-chrysostom). His works consistently encouraged a deep devotion to Holy Scripture, teaching that only those who approach it with love can fully enter into its meaning.
