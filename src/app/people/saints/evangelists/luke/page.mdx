**Evangelist** (1st century AD)

[St. Luke](/glossary/names#luke) was born, according to tradition, at Antioch in Syria, and was known to [St. Paul](/people/saints/apostles/paul) as "the beloved physician" (Col. 4:14). Although the circumstances of his conversion are uncertain, he became a faithful companion of St. Paul, joining him at Troas during his second missionary journey and accompanying him into Macedonia (Acts 16:10). He remained for some years at Philippi before rejoining the Apostle on his third missionary journey, traveling with him through Asia Minor and Palestine to Jerusalem (Acts 20:5). Luke later accompanied Paul on his voyage to Rome and remained with him during his imprisonment, continuing faithfully at his side even when others had departed (2 Tim. 4:11). He is traditionally recognized as the author of the [Gospel of Luke](/bible/new-testament/gospels/luke) and the [Acts of the Apostles](/bible/new-testament/acts), preserving both the life and teachings of Christ and the history of the apostolic Church. After the death of St. Paul, traditions place his preaching in various regions, including Macedonia, Achaia, and Italy. The circumstances of his death remain uncertain, though some ancient writers maintain that he suffered martyrdom at Patras.
