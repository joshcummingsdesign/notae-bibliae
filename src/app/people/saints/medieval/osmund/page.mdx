**Bishop** (1099 AD)

[St. Osmund](/glossary/names#osmund) was born into a noble Norman family and came to England following the Norman Conquest of 1066. He entered the king’s service, becoming Lord Chancellor before being appointed the second Bishop of Salisbury in 1078. A gifted administrator and devout churchman, he inherited a newly formed diocese and devoted himself to establishing its spiritual and institutional foundations.

Osmund’s first great achievement was overseeing the construction of the cathedral at <Definition lang="english" anchor="sarum" text="Old Sarum" />, which was consecrated on April 5, 1092. Built within the royal fortress overlooking the surrounding countryside, it became the mother church of the diocese. The cathedral remained the seat of the Bishop of Salisbury until the see was transferred to its present-day location in 1220.

His second great achievement was the organization of the cathedral chapter. Following the Norman model, he established a community of secular canons whose duties in worship, education, and pastoral ministry were carefully ordered. Its school gained a reputation for learning, while its canons became renowned for their excellence in music and liturgy, serving as a model for other English cathedrals. His third great achievement was the formation of the [Sarum Use](/liturgy/rites/sarum-rite), arranging the liturgy and services of his diocese into a form that spread throughout much of England, Wales, and Ireland, profoundly shaping the worship of the medieval English Church.

Saint Osmund died on December 4, 1099, and was buried at Old Sarum. On July 23, 1457, his remains were translated to the Lady Chapel of the present Salisbury Cathedral, where a magnificent shrine was erected before its destruction during the English Reformation under Henry VIII. Today he is remembered chiefly for the cathedral he helped build, the cathedral chapter he organized, and the Sarum Use that shaped English worship for centuries.
