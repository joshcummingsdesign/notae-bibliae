**Virgin** (1253 AD)

[St. Clare](/glossary/names#clare) was born into a noble family at Assisi in 1194 and, inspired by the preaching and example of [St. Francis of Assisi](/people/saints/medieval/francis-of-assisi), left her former life at the age of eighteen to embrace poverty and religious devotion. After receiving the habit from St. Francis, she was eventually established with her sister Agnes at San Damiano, where other women joined them, including her sister Beatrice and later their mother, Ortolana. As abbess, Clare governed the community with love and care and shared St. Francis’ radical devotion to poverty, obtaining papal sanction for her community to live without possessions and solely upon alms; her order consequently became known as the Poor Clares. During an attack upon Assisi by Saracen mercenaries, the sick Clare asked to be carried to the point of danger, preceded by the Blessed Sacrament, and prayed for the protection of her sisters and the city, after which the attackers suddenly withdrew. After forty-two years of religious life, St. Clare fell gravely ill and died at San Damiano, remembered as the great female companion of the Franciscan movement.
