**Religious** (1231 AD)

[St. Elizabeth of Hungary](/glossary/names#elizabeth) was born in 1207 to King Andrew II of Hungary and was betrothed in childhood to Ludwig, Landgrave of Thuringia, whom she married in 1220. From an early age, she was devoted to prayer and charity, frequently visiting the poor and nursing the sick. Influenced by the Franciscan friars, she embraced their ideals of poverty and service to the poor. During a famine in 1225, she opened the castle granaries to feed the hungry and established a hospital at the foot of the Wartburg. According to tradition, when Ludwig questioned her about the bread she was carrying to the poor, it miraculously turned into roses. After Ludwig died of illness at Otranto, Italy, while traveling to the Holy Land on the Sixth Crusade in 1227, Elizabeth was driven from the Wartburg by his brother Henry. Though eventually granted the town of Marburg, she continued to live in poverty, establishing a hospital dedicated to [St. Francis](/people/saints/medieval/francis-of-assisi), where she personally cared for the sick and destitute. Under the severe spiritual direction of Conrad of Marburg, her already declining health deteriorated further. She died on November 17, 1231, at the age of twenty-four, and was buried in the church of her hospital.
