**Bishop** (594 AD)

[St. Gregory of Tours](/glossary/names#gregory) was born in around 538 AD at Clermont in Auvergne, into a distinguished Gallo-Roman family whose members included several bishops. His father died while he was still young, and he was brought up under the care of his uncle, St. Gallus, Bishop of Clermont, and later of Gallus’ successor, Avitus. In his youth Gregory suffered from serious illness. After seeking the intercession of [St. Martin of Tours](/people/saints/early/martin-of-tours), he recovered, and devoted himself more fully to the service of the Church.

He was ordained deacon by Avitus, and, much against his will, was chosen Bishop of Tours in 573 AD. His episcopate fell amid the civil wars and political violence of the Frankish kingdoms. Though Tours repeatedly changed hands among their rulers, he cared for the spiritual and temporal welfare of his people, defended the weak against oppression, and bore patiently the hostility of King Chilperic and his supporters. He rebuilt the cathedral church, which had burned down under his predecessor, and fostered devotion to the saints, especially St. Martin.

Gregory is remembered especially as a historian. His *History of the Franks*, written in ten books, begins with the Creation and traces the history of Gaul and the Frankish kings down to his own time. It remains the chief source for much of 6th-century Frankish history. He also wrote important hagiographical works concerning St. Martin and other holy men and women of Gaul.

After Chilperic’s death, Gregory enjoyed a period of peace under King Guntram. When Tours later passed to Childebert II, he gained the confidence of the young king and his mother, Brunehaut, who entrusted him with important missions. St. Gregory died at Tours on November 17, 594 AD. Through his faithful service as bishop and the historical record he left of his age, he is remembered as one of the great figures of early medieval Gaul and as the “Father of French History.”
