**Abbot** (1153 AD)

[St. Bernard of Clairvaux](/glossary/names#bernard) was born in 1091 near Dijon to a noble Burgundian family and entered the newly founded Cistercian monastery of Citeaux at the age of twenty-two, bringing several members of his family with him. In 1115 he founded the monastery of Clairvaux, where he and his monks embraced a life of poverty, manual labor, and strict observance of the [*Rule of St. Benedict*](/meditations/the-rule-of-saint-benedict). Bernard's extraordinary holiness, humility, and leadership soon made him the most influential abbot of his age, and he was repeatedly called from the cloister to resolve ecclesiastical disputes, oppose theological error, and preach the Second Crusade. Though grieved by the Crusade's failure, he remained steadfast in his trust in God's providence, and after a lifetime of tireless labor he died peacefully at Clairvaux in 1153 at the age of sixty-two.
