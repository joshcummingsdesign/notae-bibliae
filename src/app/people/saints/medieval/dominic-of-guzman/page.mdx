**Priest** (1221 AD)

[St. Dominic of Guzmán](/glossary/names#dominic) was born at Caleruega in Castile in 1170 and from an early age displayed remarkable learning, austerity, and charity, once selling even his treasured books to feed the poor during a famine. After studying theology at Palencia, he became a canon at Osma, where he devoted himself to prayer and the care of souls. During the [Albigensian](/glossary/historical-terms#albigensians) Crusade, Dominic sought to combat heresy through preaching rather than violence and is traditionally associated with establishing the devotion of the [Rosary](/meditations/the-history-of-the-rosary) as a simple means of teaching the mysteries of the faith and cultivating prayer among the people. In 1215 he sought approval for the Order of Preachers, or Dominicans, whose friars would be devoted especially to preaching and theological instruction; the Order was approved by Pope Innocent III and subsequently confirmed by Pope Honorius III, and rapidly spread throughout Europe. While in Rome, Dominic met [St. Francis of Assisi](/people/saints/medieval/francis-of-assisi), beginning a friendship traditionally commemorated between their two [mendicant orders](/glossary/historical-terms#mendicant-orders). St. Dominic died among his friars at Bologna on August 6, 1221, assuring them, "I may be more useful to you where I am going than I could be here," and was buried in the church of his Order there.
