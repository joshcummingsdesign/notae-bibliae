**Apostle** (c. 50 AD)

[St. Bartholomew](/glossary/names#bartholomew), who is traditionally identified with [Nathanael](/glossary/names#nathanael), was a friend of [St. Philip](/people/saints/apostles/philip) from Cana in Galilee and one of the original twelve apostles. Philip said to him, “We have found him of whom Moses in the Law and also the prophets wrote, Jesus of Nazareth, the son of Joseph” (John 1:45). Because of his careful knowledge of the Scriptures, which foretold that the Messiah would come from Bethlehem, Nathanael asked, “Can anything good come out of Nazareth?” (John 1:46). But when Philip replied, “Come and see,” he went with him. When Jesus revealed both his divine foreknowledge and his perfect knowledge of Nathanael’s heart, he recognized him as the promised Messiah, confessing, “Rabbi, you are the Son of God! You are the King of Israel!” (John 1:49). Jesus then promised that he would see greater things—even “the angels of God ascending and descending upon the Son of Man” (John 1:51). According to tradition, St. Bartholomew preached the gospel in India, and later journeyed from Hierapolis in Syria through Armenia before continuing to Derbent on the Caspian Sea, where he faithfully bore witness to Christ and ultimately suffered martyrdom.
