**Apostle** (44 AD)

[St. James](/glossary/names#james), the son of Zebedee and brother of [St. John](/people/saints/apostles/john), was a fisherman on the Sea of Galilee who, with his brother, left his father and livelihood to follow Christ (Matt. 4:21-22). Christ named the brothers *<Definition lang="greek" anchor="boanerges" text="Boanerges" />*, "Sons of Thunder", reflecting their ardent and impetuous character (Mark 3:17), and James became one of the three apostles, with Saints Peter and John, admitted to some of the most intimate moments of our Lord's ministry, including the raising of Jairus' daughter (Luke 8:51), the Transfiguration (Matt. 17:1-2), and the agony in the Garden (Matt. 26:37). After the Ascension he remained with the apostles at Jerusalem (Acts 1:12-13), and in 44 AD Herod Agrippa I had him put to death with the sword, making him the first of the Twelve to suffer martyrdom (Acts 12:1-2). [Clement of Alexandria](/people/early-christian-writers/clement-of-alexandria) relates that the man who brought James to judgment was so moved by his steadfastness that he confessed Christ and was executed alongside him, receiving from the Apostle a kiss of peace before their deaths. According to later Spanish tradition, James had preached in Spain before his martyrdom, and his remains were afterward carried there and buried at [Compostela](/meditations/st-james-and-the-scallop-shell).
