**Apostle and Evangelist** (1st century AD)

[St. Matthew](/glossary/names#matthew), also called [Levi](/glossary/names#levi) (Luke 5:27), is traditionally understood as the son of [Cleopas](/glossary/names#cleopas), one of the men who encountered Jesus on the road to Emmaus (Luke 24:18), and of Mary, the sister of [St. Joseph's](/people/saints/biblical/joseph-of-nazareth) (John 19:25), making him a kinsman of the Lord and the brother of Saints [James the Less](/people/saints/apostles/james-the-less), [Jude](/people/saints/apostles/jude), and [Simon](/people/saints/apostles/simon) (Matt. 9:9; Mark 2:14; Matt. 10:3; Matt. 13:55). He was a tax collector at Capernaum when Jesus called him to leave his worldly occupation and follow him (Matt. 9:9), and he became one of the twelve apostles (Matt. 10:3). After the Ascension, Matthew preached the Gospel in Judaea before journeying to foreign nations. Ancient traditions variously place his missionary labours in Ethiopia, Persia, and Macedonia. As an evangelist, he is remembered for the [Gospel](/bible/new-testament/gospels/matthew) that bears his name.

## Observances

- **September 21** — [Saint Matthew](/liturgy/liturgical-year/saints/saint-matthew), Apostle and Evangelist
