**Bishop and Martyr** (c. 100 AD)

[St. Clement of Rome](/glossary/names#clement) was a disciple of [St. Peter](/people/saints/apostles/peter) and perhaps also of [St. Paul](/people/saints/apostles/paul), and is traditionally identified with the Clement whom Paul mentions as a faithful fellow worker whose name is written in the Book of Life (Phil. 4:3). He became one of the earliest bishops of Rome and is remembered especially for his Epistle to the Corinthians, written in response to a schism in the church at Corinth. According to tradition, during the reign of Emperor Trajan, Clement was banished to Pontus and condemned to labor in the marble quarries, where he comforted and encouraged his fellow Christians. When the convicts suffered from a scarcity of water, Clement miraculously discovered a spring. Having converted many pagans to the Christian faith, he was condemned to be drowned in the sea with an anchor attached to his neck. His relics were later brought to Constantinople by St. Cyril, Apostle to the Slavs, and some were eventually deposited in the church of San Clemente in Rome. St. Clement is remembered as one of the Apostolic Fathers of the Church.
