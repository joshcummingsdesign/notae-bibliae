<Initial text="Zechariah the Priest" />

**1st century AD**

[Zechariah](/glossary/names#zechariah), the father of [St. John the Baptist](/people/saints/biblical/john-the-baptist), was a Levitical priest who served at the temple in Jerusalem (Luke 1:5). He and his wife [St. Elizabeth](/people/saints/biblical/elizabeth) had no children and were advanced in years (Luke 1:7). One day, Zechariah was chosen by lot to burn incense in the temple, when suddenly the angel [Gabriel](/people/saints/angels/gabriel) appeared (Luke 1:9-11). Gabriel tells him they will have a son named John who will be filled with the Holy Spirit from his mother’s womb (Luke 1:13-15) and “make ready for the Lord a people prepared” (Luke 1:17). Zechariah doubts Gabriel because he and Elizabeth are old, and he is made deaf and mute (Luke 1:18-20).

Six months later, the angel Gabriel visits the [Blessed Virgin Mary](/people/saints/biblical/the-blessed-virgin-mary) (Luke 1:26-27). He tells her that the Holy Spirit will come upon her, and that she will have a son named Jesus, the Son of the Most High (Luke 1:31-32). Mary goes to the hill country of Judea to visit Elizabeth, and baby John leaps in Elizabeth’s womb (Luke 1:39-41). Elizabeth is filled with the Holy Spirit and cries out, “Blessed are you among women, and blessed is the fruit of your womb!” (Luke 1:42), and “Blessed is she who believed that there would be a fulfillment of what was spoken to her from the Lord” (Luke 1:45).

Elizabeth gives birth to a son, and after eight days they go to have him circumcised and named (Luke 1:57-58). The midwives—who were often involved in the naming of a child (Ruth 4:17)—want to name him after his father Zechariah, which is also the name of the famous prophet, but Elizabeth, prompted by the Holy Spirit, says that he shall be called John (Luke 1:59-60). They sign to Zechariah and ask him what the baby should be called, and he writes down, “His name is John” (Luke 1:63). Immediately Zechariah’s mouth is opened and he is filled with the Holy Spirit and begins to prophesy (Luke 1:64). He prays the [*Benedictus*](/liturgy/music/chants/benedictus), praising God for raising up a Savior for whom his son will prepare the way (Luke 1:68). John grows up and is in the wilderness until his public appearance to Israel (Luke 1:80).
