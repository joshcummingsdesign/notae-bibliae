<Initial text="Simeon the Prophet" />

**1st century AD**

[St. Simeon the Prophet](/glossary/names#simeon) is described as a righteous and devout man in Jerusalem who was waiting for the coming of the Messiah (Luke 2:25). The Holy Spirit reveals to Simeon that he will not see death before his coming (Luke 2:26). After Jesus' birth, Mary fulfills the rite of purification according to the Law (Lev. 12:2-8). After the 40 days, Mary and Joseph bring Jesus to the temple in Jerusalem to make their offering (Luke 2:22-24), and the Spirit sends Simeon into the temple (Luke 2:27). Simeon takes Jesus into his arms, blesses God, and recites the [Nunc dimittis](/liturgy/music/chants/nunc-dimittis): "Lord, now lettest thou thy  servant depart in peace, according to thy word..." (Luke 2:29-32). Mary and Joseph marvel at his words (Luke 2:33). Simeon blesses them and says to Mary, "Behold, this child is appointed for the fall and rising of many in Israel, and for a sign that is opposed (and a sword will pierce through your own soul also), so that thoughts from many hearts may be revealed" (Luke 2:34-35).
