**1st century AD**

[St. Mary Magdalene](/glossary/names#mary), so called because she was from Magdala on the western shore of the Sea of Galilee, was a devoted follower of Christ, from whom he had cast seven demons, and she thereafter accompanied him with other women who ministered to him from their own means (Luke 8:1-3). She remained faithful through his Passion, standing near the cross (John 19:25), witnessing his burial, and returning with the other women to the tomb with spices after the Sabbath (Luke 24:1). After [St. Peter](/people/saints/apostles/peter) and [St. John](/people/saints/apostles/john) had visited the empty tomb and departed, Mary remained behind weeping and saw two angels sitting where the body of Jesus had lain, one at the head and one at the feet (John 20:11-12). The risen Christ then appeared to her and called her by name, and she was sent to announce to the disciples, "I have seen the Lord" (John 20:14-18). Traditionally identified with the sinful woman who anointed Christ and washed his feet with her tears (Luke 7:36-38), Mary is remembered especially for her devoted love of Christ and her perseverance in seeking him, through which she became the first recorded witness to proclaim the Resurrection to the apostles.
