**Bishop** (638 AD)

[St. Sophronius of Jerusalem](/glossary/names#sophronius) was born in around 560 in Damascus and received a thorough education in Greek rhetoric, philosophy, and theology. Drawn to the ascetic life, he entered the Monastery of Saint Theodosius near Bethlehem, where he formed a close friendship with the monk John Moschus. Together they traveled widely through the monasteries of Palestine, Egypt, and North Africa, encouraging the faithful during a time of theological controversy. After Moschus' death, Sophronius returned to Palestine, bringing with him his companion's spiritual legacy.

Sophronius became one of the leading defenders of the orthodox doctrine that Christ possesses both a divine and a human will. At a time when the heresy of [Monothelitism](/glossary/historical-terms#monothelitism) claimed that Christ had only one will, he argued that Christ's complete humanity required a true human will united in perfect harmony with his divine will. His theological clarity helped prepare the way for the Church's later affirmation of this doctrine at the Third Council of Constantinople (680–681).

In about 634, Sophronius was elected Patriarch of Jerusalem. His patriarchate coincided with one of the most difficult periods in the city's history, as Muslim armies advanced through the Holy Land. After a prolonged siege, Jerusalem surrendered in 637 to the Caliph Umar ibn al-Khattab. According to tradition, Sophronius personally negotiated the city's peaceful surrender, seeking to preserve the lives of its inhabitants and the sanctity of its holy places, though he lamented the loss of Christian rule.

Sophronius died around 638, shortly after the fall of Jerusalem. He is remembered not only as a courageous shepherd during a time of political upheaval but also as a gifted theologian, hymnwriter, and spiritual writer. Among his extant works are his sermons, the *Synodical Letter*, which defends the two wills of Christ, and the *Life of St. Mary of Egypt*, one of the most beloved spiritual biographies in the Christian East. His steadfast defense of the two wills of Christ secured his place among the Greek Fathers of the Church.
