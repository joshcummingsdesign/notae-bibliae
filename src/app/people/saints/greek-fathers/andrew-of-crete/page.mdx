**Bishop** (c. 740 AD)

[St. Andrew of Crete](/glossary/names#andrew) was born around 650 in Damascus to Christian parents. According to tradition, he was unable to speak until the age of seven, when he received Holy Communion and miraculously found his voice. As a youth he entered the monastery of Mar Saba near Jerusalem, where he devoted himself to prayer, study, and the Scriptures. His learning and holiness soon brought him into the service of the Church of Jerusalem, and he was later sent to Constantinople to represent the patriarch at the Third Council of Constantinople (680–681), which condemned [Monothelitism](/glossary/historical-terms#monothelitism) and affirmed that Christ possesses both a divine and a human will.

After the council Andrew remained in Constantinople, where he served as a deacon at the great church of Hagia Sophia and cared for orphanages and charitable institutions. Around the end of the 7th century he was appointed Archbishop of Crete, where he faithfully shepherded his flock through preaching, pastoral care, and the building of churches. His episcopate was marked by a deep concern for both sound doctrine and the spiritual growth of the faithful.

Andrew is remembered as one of the greatest hymnwriters in the Eastern Church. He helped develop the canon, a poetic form of liturgical hymnody that became central to Byzantine worship. His masterpiece, the *Great Canon*, is a profound meditation on repentance that weaves together examples from Scripture and is still chanted during Lent in the Eastern Church. Many of his sermons are still extant and display both theological depth and rhetorical beauty.

Near the end of his life, Andrew traveled to Constantinople on ecclesiastical business. While returning to Crete, he died around 740 on the Greek island of Lesbos. Revered in both the Eastern and Western Churches, he is remembered not only for his defense of the orthodox faith but also for his enduring contribution to Christian worship. Through his hymns and sermons, he continues to call believers to repentance, holiness, and joyful praise of God.
