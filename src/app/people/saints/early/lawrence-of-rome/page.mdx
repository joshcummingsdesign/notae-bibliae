**Deacon and Martyr** (258 AD)

[St. Lawrence](/glossary/names#lawrence) was the archdeacon of Pope St. Sixtus II at Rome during the persecution of Emperor Valerian. When Sixtus was led away to martyrdom, Lawrence cried after him, asking why his bishop should go to the sacrifice without his attendant deacon, and Sixtus foretold that Lawrence would follow him within three days. As keeper of the Church’s possessions, Lawrence was arrested and ordered to surrender its treasures; instead, he gathered the poor, the lame, and the blind and presented them to the authorities, declaring, “These are the treasures of the Church.” He was condemned to a cruel death, traditionally said to have been burned upon a gridiron, and endured his suffering without complaint until his death. [St. Augustine](/people/saints/latin-fathers/augustine-of-hippo) later praised Lawrence as a model of Christian charity and martyrdom: as a deacon he had ministered the Blood of Christ, and in martyrdom he poured out his own blood for the sake of Christ’s Name.
