**Virgin and Martyr** (c. 305 AD)

[St. Catherine of Alexandria](/glossary/names#catherine) was the daughter of a king and a Christian virgin of remarkable beauty and learning. When the emperor summoned fifty philosophers to dispute with her publicly, Catherine convinced them all of the truth of the Christian faith. Enraged by their conversion, the emperor ordered them to be put to death. Having refused the emperor's advances and his offer to share his throne, she was scourged and imprisoned. During her imprisonment, she converted the emperor's wife, Faustina, and an attendant named Porphyrius, who in turn converted two hundred soldiers. All were subsequently put to death for their faith. Catherine was condemned to suffer death upon a wheel fitted with razors, but the wheel miraculously broke when she was placed upon it. She then prayed that her body might be preserved from desecration after her death and that the world might be converted to Christianity. According to legend, she was beheaded, and her body was carried by angels to Mount Sinai.
