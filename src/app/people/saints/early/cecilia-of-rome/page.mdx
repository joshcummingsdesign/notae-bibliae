**Virgin and Martyr** (3rd century AD)

[St. Cecilia](/glossary/names#cecilia) was a Christian virgin of Rome who was given in marriage to a young man named Valerian. According to tradition, she had consecrated her virginity to God and persuaded her husband to receive baptism from Pope Urban. Upon returning home, Valerian saw an angel beside Cecilia, who crowned them both with roses and lilies. Valerian's brother, Tibertius, was likewise converted, and the two brothers devoted themselves to burying Christian martyrs. When they were arrested and refused to sacrifice to the Roman gods, they were executed with the sword. Cecilia continued to preach the Gospel, converting many to the Christian faith before she herself was arrested and condemned to be suffocated in a heated bath chamber. When she miraculously survived, an executioner was sent to behead her, but failed to complete the execution. She lived for three more days, devoting her remaining hours to preaching and prayer before her death. She was buried by Pope Urban and his deacons. St. Cecilia is remembered as the patroness of music.
