**Bishop** (401 AD)

[St. Martin of Tours](/glossary/names#martin) was born at Sabaria in Pannonia (modern-day Szombathely, Hungary) to pagan parents and entered the Roman army at the age of fifteen. While serving as a soldier, he famously divided his cloak with a poor beggar and afterward saw Christ in a vision wearing the garment, leading to his baptism at Amiens. Having renounced military service, he became a disciple of [St. Hilary of Poitiers](/people/saints/latin-fathers/hilary-of-poitiers) and embraced the monastic life, eventually founding a monastery at Ligugé. Around 371 AD, he was chosen Bishop of Tours, though he continued to live as a monk, establishing another monastic community at Marmoutier. Renowned for his miracles, preaching, and efforts to convert the pagan inhabitants of Gaul, Martin also firmly opposed the use of secular violence against heretics, particularly during the persecution of Priscillian and his followers. His humility, charity, and devotion to the Church made him one of the most revered bishops of his age. He died at Candes on November 9, 401 AD, and was buried at Tours on November 11, where his tomb became an important place of pilgrimage.
