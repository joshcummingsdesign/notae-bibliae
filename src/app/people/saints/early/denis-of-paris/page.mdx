**Bishop and Martyr** (c. 286 AD)

[St. Denis](/glossary/names#denis) was sent to Gaul to establish a church and came to Lutetia Parisiorum, the city later known as Paris, where he preached the Gospel and won converts to the Christian faith. According to tradition, he endured many persecutions, being exposed to wild beasts, cast into a fiery furnace, and crucified, yet miraculously surviving. At length, he and his companions, Rusticus and Eleutherius, his deacon and subdeacon, were imprisoned and beheaded on the hill afterward known as Montmartre, or "Martyrs' Mount." A later tradition relates that Denis rose after his execution, took his head in his hands, and carried it, accompanied by a choir of angels, to the place where the Abbey of Saint-Denis was afterward founded. His memory was preserved in the writings of [St. Gregory of Tours](/people/saints/medieval/gregory-of-tours), and the abbey became an important center of devotion, housing the relics of Denis and his companions.
