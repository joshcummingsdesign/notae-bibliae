**Priest and Martyr** (c. 235 AD)

[St. Hippolytus of Rome](/glossary/names#hippolytus) was a learned presbyter of the Church at Rome and leader of its Greek-speaking Christians, renowned for his eloquence and theological learning. A defender of the *<Definition lang="greek" anchor="logos" text="Logos" />* doctrine taught by the Greek apologists, especially [St. Justin Martyr](/people/saints/greek-fathers/justin-martyr), he became the leader of a rigorist party and was elected as a rival bishop in opposition to the Roman popes over the discipline of penitence.

Despite this division, Hippolytus and the Roman Church were eventually reconciled. During the persecution under Emperor Maximinus Thrax, he was exiled to the mines of Sardinia, where he is believed to have died in around 235 AD.

Hippolytus was among the most prolific Christian writers of the 3rd century, producing works of biblical interpretation, apologetics, homilies, ecclesiastical law, and chronology. His best-preserved writings include the *Commentary on the Prophet Daniel* and the *Commentary on the Song of Songs*, the latter being the earliest surviving Christian commentary on that book.

He is also traditionally associated with the *Apostolic Tradition*, an early treatise on church order that preserves invaluable information about the life, worship, and ministry of the Church in the 3rd century. Among its most important features are its description of <Definition lang="greek" anchor="katekhesis" text="catechesis" /> and the earliest known account of Christian ordination, making it one of the most significant sources for understanding the liturgy and practice of the early church.
