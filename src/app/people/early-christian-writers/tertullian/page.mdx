[Tertullian](/glossary/names#tertullian) was born around 155 AD in Carthage, in the Roman province of Africa, and received an excellent classical education. He converted to Christianity as an adult, describing conversion as a decisive transformation and famously declaring that "Christians are made, not born." His writings also indicate that he was married to a Christian wife.

As the first Christian author to produce an extensive body of theological works in Latin, Tertullian became known as the "Father of Latin Christianity" and the "Founder of Western Theology." His writings covered a wide range of subjects, including apologetics, church polity, discipline, Christian morals, and the refutation of heresy, providing valuable insight into the life and thought of the early Church.

Tertullian made significant contributions to the development of Trinitarian theology, introducing the Latin term *<Definition lang="latin" anchor="trinitas" text="Trinitas" />* and describing God as one substance in three persons. He also emphasized the full divinity and humanity of Christ and wrote forcefully against heresies such as [Marcionism](/glossary/historical-terms#marcionism), [Gnosticism](/glossary/historical-terms#gnosticism), and [Monarchianism](/glossary/historical-terms#monarchianism). His *Apologeticus* remains one of the earliest and most influential defenses of Christianity against Roman persecution and pagan criticism.

Known for his rigorous moral standards and ascetic outlook, Tertullian emphasized personal holiness and separation from the corruptions of pagan society. His writings profoundly influenced later Christian thinkers, including [St. Cyprian](/people/saints/latin-fathers/cyprian-of-carthage), [St. Augustine](/people/saints/latin-fathers/augustine-of-hippo), and even Reformers such as [John Calvin](/meditations/a-brief-history-of-the-reformation-part-3#john-calvin), securing his place as one of the foundational figures in the development of Christian theology and Western thought.
