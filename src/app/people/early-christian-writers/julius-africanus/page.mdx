<Initial text="Julius Africanus" />

[Sextus Julius Africanus](/glossary/names#julius) was a Christian traveler and historian of the late 2nd and early 3rd centuries. Probably a native of Jerusalem, he lived at nearby Emmaus and was well acquainted with the geography of Judea. His historical writings would later influence Eusebius and other Christian chroniclers.

Africanus may have served under Emperor Septimius Severus during the campaign against the Osroenians in 195 AD. He later went on an embassy to Emperor Severus Alexander to petition for the restoration of the ruined city of Emmaus. The mission succeeded, and the city was thereafter known as Nicopolis.

A learned scholar, Africanus traveled widely throughout the Roman Empire, visiting Greece, Rome, and Alexandria, where he studied at the renowned catechetical school. He was proficient in Greek, Latin, and Hebrew, equipping him for his historical and literary work.

Africanus is best known for his *Chronographiai*, a five-volume history of the world. Though the work survives only in fragments, it profoundly influenced [Eusebius'](/people/early-christian-writers/eusebius) *Ecclesiastical History* and was highly regarded by later Christian writers, including [St. Jerome](/people/saints/latin-fathers/jerome-of-stridon), who cites it in his *Commentary on Daniel*.
