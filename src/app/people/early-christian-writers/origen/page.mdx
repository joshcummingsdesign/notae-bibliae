[Origen](/glossary/names#origen) was born around 185 AD into a devout Christian family in Alexandria and received an excellent education in Greek literature and Holy Scripture. After his father Leonides was martyred during the persecution under Septimius Severus, Origen devoted himself to supporting his impoverished family. At the age of eighteen he was appointed a catechist at the Catechetical School of Alexandria, where his learning, asceticism, and zeal quickly gained him a wide reputation.

Origen became one of the most prolific Christian scholars of the early church, producing extensive biblical commentaries, theological treatises, and apologetic works. His writings, including *On the First Principles*, the *Hexapla* (a critical edition of the Hebrew Bible in six versions), and numerous commentaries on Scripture, exercised an enormous influence on Christian theology and biblical interpretation. After conflicts with Demetrius, Bishop of Alexandria, Origen settled in Caesarea, where he founded a renowned Christian school and continued teaching and writing.

Revered throughout the East as one of the foremost Christian intellectuals of his age, Origen devoted his life to the study of philosophy and theology, seeking to explain the Christian faith to both believers and inquirers. He instructed generations of students, preached regularly, and wrote *Exhortation to Martyrdom* during the persecution under Emperor Maximinus Thrax. Later, under the persecution of Emperor Decius, he endured imprisonment and torture rather than renounce Christ, dying shortly after his release around 253 AD.

Origen's influence on Christian theology, especially in the East, was immense. Although some of his theological opinions gave rise to later controversy and several of his teachings were condemned in the 6th century, his scholarship profoundly shaped the development of biblical exegesis and Christian thought, leaving a legacy that continued to influence the Church for centuries.
