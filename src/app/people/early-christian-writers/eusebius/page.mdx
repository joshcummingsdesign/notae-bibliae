[Eusebius](/glossary/names#eusebius) was born around 250 AD in or near Caesarea Maritima, where he was baptized, educated, and later ordained a presbyter. He became a disciple of Pamphilus, inheriting the scholarly tradition established by [Origen](/people/early-christian-writers/origen). Under Pamphilus' guidance, Eusebius helped expand the great library of Caesarea, whose unparalleled collections laid the foundation for his lifelong study of Scripture, history, and theology.

In the 290s, Eusebius began composing his *Ecclesiastical History*, the first comprehensive history of the Christian Church from the apostolic age to his own time. He also wrote his *Chronicle*, a universal history of the world. Drawing upon the rich resources of the Caesarean library—including Origen's writings and research into the books used by the churches—Eusebius preserved invaluable information about the history, literature, and Scriptures of the early church.

Around 313 AD, Eusebius became Bishop of Caesarea. During the [Arian](/glossary/historical-terms#arianism) controversy, Arius sought his support after his condemnation by Alexander of Alexandria, and Eusebius later played a prominent role at the Council of Nicaea in 325 AD, where he presented the creed of the Church of Caesarea. Although the council adopted the Nicene Creed, Eusebius remained a trusted adviser to Emperor Constantine and later wrote the *Life of Constantine*, an important historical work based in part on eyewitness testimony and primary sources.

Eusebius was one of the most learned Christian scholars of late antiquity. In addition to his *Ecclesiastical History*, he wrote *Preparations for the Gospel*, *Demonstrations of the Gospel*, *On Discrepancies between the Gospels*, and the *Onomasticon* ("Of Names"), an early geographical lexicon of biblical places. His careful preservation of the history, writings, and traditions of the early church earned him the title "Father of Church History."
