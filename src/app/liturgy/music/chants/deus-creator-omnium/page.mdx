## History

A Latin hymn by [St. Ambrose](/people/saints/latin-fathers/ambrose-of-milan) written in the 4th century. The hymn is sung at [Vespers](/glossary/liturgical-terms#vespers) in the Liturgy of the Hours on Lord's Days.

## Text

<Poetry>
|  \b Deus, Creator omnium,
|  \b Polique Rector, vestiens
|  \b Diem decoro lumine,
|  \b Noctem soporis gratia.
|
|  O God, Creator of all things,
|  And Ruler of the heavens,
|  Clothing the day with gracious light,
|  And the night with the gift of sleep.
|
|  \b Artus solutos ut quies
|  \b Reddat laboris usui,
|  \b Mentesque fessas allevet,
|  \b Luctusque solvat anxios;
|
|  That rest may restore our wearied limbs
|  For the labour of the morrow,
|  Refresh our tired minds,
|  And free us from anxious grief.
|
|  \b Grates, peracto jam die
|  \b Et, noctis exortu, preces,
|  \b Voti reos ut adjuves,
|  \b Hymnum canentes solvimus.
|
|  With the day now ended,
|  And night beginning, we offer
|  Our thanks and prayers in song,
|  That thou mayest help us fulfil our vows.
|
|  \b Te cordis ima concinant,
|  \b Te vox sonora concrepet,
|  \b Te diligat castus amor,
|  \b Te mens adoret sobria;
|
|  May the depths of our hearts sing of thee,
|  Our voices resound thy praise,
|  Pure love cherish thee,
|  And sober minds adore thee.
|
|  \b Ut, cum profunda clauserit
|  \b Diem caligo noctium,
|  \b Fides tenebras nesciat,
|  \b Et nox fide reluceat.
|
|  That, when the deep darkness of night
|  Has closed the day,
|  Faith may know no darkness,
|  And the night may shine with faith.
|
|  \b Dormire mentem ne sinas
|  \b Dormire culpa noverit:
|  \b Castos fides refrigerans,
|  \b Somni vaporem temperet.
|
|  Do not suffer the mind to sleep;
|  Let sin alone know sleep.
|  May faith, which keeps us chaste and cool,
|  Temper the heat of sleep.
|
|  \b Exuta sensu lubrico
|  \b Te cordis alta somnient,
|  \b Ne hostis invidi dolo
|  \b Pavor quietos suscitet.
|
|  Freed from sensual thoughts,
|  May the depths of our hearts dream of thee;
|  Nor let fear, by the wiles
|  Of the envious enemy, rouse those at rest.
|
|  \b Christum rogemus et Patrem,
|  \b Christi Patrisque Spiritum:
|  \b Unum potens per omnia,
|  \b Fove precantes, Trinitas.
|
|  Let us beseech Christ and the Father,
|  And the Spirit of Christ and the Father:
|  O Trinity, one and mighty in all things,
|  Cherish us as we pray.
|
|  \b Deo Patri sit gloria,
|  \b Ejusque soli Filio,
|  \b Cum Spiritu Paraclito,
|  \b In sempiterna saecula. Amen.
|
|  Glory be to God the Father,
|  And to his only Son,
|  With the Spirit, the Comforter,
|  Through everlasting ages. Amen.
</Poetry>

[^1]: "Deus Creator omnium", [*Book of Vespers According to the Rite of the Holy Church of Milan*](https://cantoambrosiano.altervista.org/musica/LiberVesperalisAmbrosiano.pdf) (1939), 2–3
