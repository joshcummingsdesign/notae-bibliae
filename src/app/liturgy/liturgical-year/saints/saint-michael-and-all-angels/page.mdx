<Initial text="Saint Michael and all Angels" />

**September 29**<br/>
*Feast*

## Morning Prayer

The angels are pure spirits created by God to love and serve him. According to Pseudo-Dionysius the Areopagite, they are arranged in the following hierarchy:

**I. Universal Providence**
    - Seraphim
    - Cherubim
    - Thrones

**II. General Providence**
    - Dominions
    - Virtues
    - Powers

**III. Particular Providence**
    - Principalities
    - Archangels
    - Angels

The festival of [St. Michael](/people/saints/angels/michael) and all Angels opens to us a vision of heaven inhabited by a vast multitude. All is order; all is harmony. Yet in this very order there is variety, and harmony implies diversity. No existences, as they leave the hand of God, are identical. If God be the God of concord, he is the God of variety as well. The angelic host follows this rule. Each angel differs from his fellow, as each man differs from his fellow, as each beast or each flower is various. But, though different, they are arranged in distinctive groups or families, or orders. Of these orders there are three, and in each of these three orders there are three choirs.

The first order contains seraphim, cherubim, and thrones, and is engaged in God’s immediate presence, in the act of constant adoration. The second order contains dominions, virtues, and powers, and is engaged in the struggle which is constantly going on with evil. The third order contains principalities, archangels, and angels, and is engaged in the care of creation. Now each of these choirs has its appointed office, into the details of which, as gathered out of Holy Writ, there is not space to enter here; but a few words will show how they all unite in ministering to man, for every angel is a ministering spirit—every one without exception (cf. Heb. 1:14). Thus a seraph was sent to Isaiah (Isa. 6:6), a cherub to guard Paradise (Gen. 3:24); Michael, the prince of the order of principalities, was dispatched by God to Daniel (Dan. 10:13); [St. Gabriel the Archangel](/people/saints/angels/gabriel) to [Mary](/people/saints/biblical/the-blessed-virgin-mary) (Luke 1:26-27); [Raphael](/glossary/names#raphael), the chief of virtues, to Tobit (Tobit 12:15).

All, in their ministry to God, minister to us men. Angels are our guardians; archangels instruct us in the divine law and in celestial mysteries; principalities govern our life aright; powers drive far from us the deadly foe; virtues attend to our health, spiritual and corporal; dominions give us strength to rule over our inordinate passions; thrones establish us in good we have begun; cherubim illumine the mind with celestial wisdom; seraphim inflame the heart with the ardour of heavenly love. Yet these orders of angels do not act independently of God, but God acts mediately through them in the work of our preservation and restoration. In the seraphim it is God who burns with love; in the cherubim it is God who enlightens with wisdom; in the thrones it is God who sits in equity; in dominions it is God who rules in majesty; in virtues it is he who operates in healing the nations; in powers it is he who excels in strength; in principalities it is he who reigns as Prince; in archangels it is he who beams as Light of light; in angels it is he who sends.

## Evening Prayer

Little do we know, as we look upon the course of nature, how intimately the angelic powers are connected with its permanency. In Revelation we are told of an angel having power over the fire (Rev. 14:18); of another angel, over the waters (Rev. 16:5); of four holding the winds (Rev. 7:1). Angels fight for us against Satan, as Michael fought with the dragon (Rev. 12:7). Angels guard us in peril, as the angel of God stopped the mouths of the lions when Daniel was cast into their den (Dan. 6:22). Angels provide for the necessities of man, as an angel showed Hagar and Ishmael a fountain where they might drink (Gen. 21:17). Angels offer up our prayers to God, as Raphael says to Tobias: “When thou didst pray (and Sara, thy daughter-in-law), I did bring the remembrance of your prayers before the Holy One. … I am Raphael, one of the seven holy angels which present the prayers of the saints, and which go in and out before the glory of the Holy One” (Tobit 12:12-15).

Another office of angels is the guardianship of the Church. These are the watchmen of whom Isaiah speaks as being set upon the walls of Jerusalem, never to hold their peace day and night (Isa. 62:6). These are they who, like as the mountains stand about Jerusalem, stand in the power of the Lord round about his people (Ps. 125:2). These are they whom the servant of Elisha beheld when his eyes were opened, and saw the mountain covered with chariots and horses of fire (2 Kings 6:17). Michael is the chief of these guardians. He was prince of the armies which stood round about the Jewish people. Now he is prince of the host guarding the Church of God.

The Fathers of the Church hold that angels were created on the first day, and that the devils fell when God divided the light from the darkness. The Book of Revelation gives us to understand that a third part of the heavenly host were involved in the apostasy of Satan (Rev. 12:4). Man was created, it is supposed, to fill the void caused by the fall of the evil angels, and hence the animosity borne towards him by the devils. The number of the elect is the number of the empty thrones, principalities, powers, and dominions in heaven. The evil spirits know that man will occupy the seats they have lost, and intense is their envy and jealousy accordingly. But the good angels rejoice in the making up of that number, when all the heavenly courts will be filled again, and there will be no gaps in the ranks of the beatified. To every baptized man the place and the crown of a fallen spirit is offered, but all will not accept the offer—as in the parable of the great supper, one by one makes excuse (Luke 14:15-24); and the sceptre, the crown, and the throne are no more held out to him; they are offered to some poor penitent, or some poor heathen man, who through the darkness of his ignorance seeks God, if haply he might feel after him and find him.

Frequent allusion is made in Holy Scripture to the seven holy angels who stand in the presence of God (Rev. 8:2; Rev. 15:1; Rev. 16:1; Tobit 12:15). Four of these only are given names by the Church: Saints Michael (Dan. 10:13), Gabriel (Dan. 8:16), Raphael (Tobit 12:15), and [Uriel](/glossary/names#uriel) (2 Esdras 4:1). The others are, however, thus distinguished by the Jews: Chamuel, who wrestled with Jacob (Gen. 32:24); Jophiel, who expelled Adam and Eve from Paradise (Gen. 3:24); and Zadkiel, who held back the hand of Abraham from slaying his son (Gen. 22:11-12). The seven angels, without being distinguished by name, are occasionally introduced into Christian art, as in pictures of the Last Judgment, and at the Crucifixion, where they bear the several instruments of the Passion.[^1]
