The <Definition lang="latin" anchor="usus" text="Use of" /> <Definition lang="english" anchor="sarum" text="Sarum" /> was the local form of the Latin liturgy developed at Salisbury Cathedral under the influence of [St. Osmund](/people/saints/medieval/osmund-of-salisbury) in the late 11th century and used throughout much of medieval England until the English Reformation. While rooted in the [Roman Rite](/liturgy/traditions/roman), it was distinguished by its own customs of ceremony, chant, and calendar. Today, the Use of Sarum remains the principal witness to the liturgical life of the pre-Reformation English Church.
