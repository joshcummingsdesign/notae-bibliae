Ancient Greece had several regional dialects, including Ionic, Aeolic, Doric, and Attic, the dialect of Athens. The earliest Greek poets on record composed either in a stylized amalgam of Old Ionic and other dialects, as did Homer and Hesiod, or in Aeolic, as did Sappho and Alcaeus. Athens' rise as a political and cultural center made Attic a prestigious literary language. The Macedonian kings adopted it, and Alexander the Great's conquests in the fourth century BC spread Greek across the eastern Mediterranean and the Middle East. The need for communication among speakers of different dialects encouraged the development of <Definition lang="greek" anchor="koine" text="Koine" />, or "common" Greek, based primarily on Attic with significant Ionic influence. It remained the region's lingua franca for trade, administration, and scholarship for centuries after Alexander's empire fragmented.
