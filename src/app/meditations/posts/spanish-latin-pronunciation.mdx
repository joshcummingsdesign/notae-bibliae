If Latin pronunciation were simply a matter of taste, I would choose Spanish Latin pronunciation. To my ear, it is unparalleled when you hear it sung in the choir. It evokes the image of [St. Dominic](/people/saints/medieval/dominic-of-guzman) chanting at the Cathedral of Osma in Spain. The following is a quick reference guide to Spanish Latin pronunciation:

- **a, e, i, o, u** sound as they do in the International Phonetic Alphabet (IPA).
- **y** sounds like **i**.
- **ae/oe** sounds like **e**.
- **u** as a consonant (also written as **v**) makes the fricative "v" sound.
- **ci/ce** sounds like “tsi” and “tse”, respectively.
- **ch** before a vowel sounds like “ch”, as in “cherubim”.
- **c** is otherwise hard.
- **g** is always hard.
- **ph** sounds like "f".
- **h** is otherwise silent.
- **r** is trilled.
- **ti** before another vowel becomes “tsi”.
- **t** is otherwise hard.[^1]

[^1]: Timothy J. McGee, A. G. Rigg, and David N. Klausner, eds., [*Singing Early Music*](https://archive.org/details/singingearlymusi0000unse/page/162/mode/2up) (Bloomington, IN: Indiana University Press, 2004), 163–165
