August 15 has long been observed as a [Marian](/people/saints/biblical/blessed-virgin-mary) feast, however, through the centuries it has been known by different titles. The Eastern Church refers to it as the <Definition lang="latin" anchor="dormitionem" text="Dormition" /> of the Most Holy *<Definition lang="greek" anchor="theotokos" text="Theotokos" />*, confessing both the <Definition lang="latin" anchor="repauso" text="repose" /> and the bodily <Definition lang="latin" anchor="assumptio" text="assumption" /> of Mary.[^1] The Roman Catholic Church refers to it as the Assumption of the Blessed Virgin Mary, affirming her bodily assumption without requiring an explicit affirmation of her repose—though many Roman Catholics would affirm her repose as well.[^2] The historical basis for these views is drawn from such sources as the *<Definition lang="latin" anchor="transitus-mariae" text="Transitus Mariae" />*—a collection of apocryphal texts from the 4th and 5th centuries concerning the end of Mary's earthly life—as well as the absence of any claimed bodily relics of the Virgin Mary.[^3]

In his 1522 *Sermon on the Assumption of Mary*, Martin Luther affirms that Mary and the saints are indeed in heaven, but cautions us to avoid further theological definition where Scripture is silent.

> "One cannot determine how Mary is in heaven, nor is it necessary, even if we cannot fully comprehend how matters stand with the saints in heaven. It is enough for us to know that they live in Christ, as God says in Matthew 22: 'He is not the God of the dead, but of the living' (Matt. 22:32), proving it from Exodus 3: 'I am the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob' (Exod. 3:6). His conclusion is this: if he is the God of Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob, then they must indeed be living. Therefore these passages should be sufficient for us to know that they live. But how they live we should not pry into, since it is not necessary for us to know ... I say this so that our faith may always rest upon what is certain, and that we may not make articles of faith where we have no Scripture for them. Otherwise, new articles of faith would arise day after day. Therefore make a great distinction between those things that are necessary to believe and those that are not. Always hold fast to what Scripture clearly teaches, for where Scripture does not speak plainly, do not build. But here Scripture plainly says that Abraham, Isaac, Jacob, and all believers live. Therefore it is necessary for you to believe that the Mother of God lives. But how she lives, leave that to the dear God."
>
> <cite>[Luther, WA 10/3:268–269](https://www.maartenluther.com/10%20III%20Predigten%201522.pdf)</cite>
